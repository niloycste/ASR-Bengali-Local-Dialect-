\paragraph{Key findings.}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Zero-shot models fail on dialectal CS.}
        Whisper large-v3, despite 680k~hours of pre-training, reaches only
        0.91~WER / 0.76~CER, and the same-size Whisper-small is worse
        (1.07~WER). Multilingual pre-training alone does not transfer to
        dialectal code-switched Bengali.
  \item \textbf{Fine-tuning on BanglaMix helps substantially.}
        Fine-tuning Whisper-small on the dataset lowers WER from 1.07 to 0.80
        (CER 1.02$\rightarrow$0.49), a 25\% relative WER reduction on an
        identical architecture --- isolating the value of the data from model
        scale.
  \item \textbf{The proposed model is the strongest system.}
        Whisper~FT~ALL attains 0.80~WER / 0.49~CER, significantly better than
        every baseline ($p<0.001$, paired bootstrap; Section~\ref{sec:analysis}),
        including the fine-tuned CTC model (0.86~WER) and zero-shot large-v3
        (0.91~WER).
  \item \textbf{Ablation --- code-switched data improves CS handling.}
        BN\_ONLY matches ALL on overall WER (0.80) but barely recognises
        English (CS-F1 0.01, WER-EN 1.00); CS\_ONLY best preserves English
        (WER-EN 0.76, CS-F1 0.28) but sacrifices overall accuracy (0.88~WER).
        Training on ALL clips gives the best \emph{balance} --- top overall
        accuracy while markedly improving English-word and code-switch metrics.
  \item \textbf{Absolute error remains high}, reflecting the inherent
        difficulty of un-standardised dialectal orthography and silver-standard
        labels; BanglaMix is positioned as a challenging open benchmark
        (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\end{itemize}
