\paragraph{English word survival.}
A clear pattern emerges on code-switched clips: monolingual CTC models (MMS,
wav2vec2; EN survival 0.00) and the Bengali-only ablation (0.01) essentially
\emph{never} reproduce English words, deleting them or mapping them to Bengali
script. Code-switch training raises this sharply --- the CS\_ONLY ablation
reaches 0.37 EN survival, the best among fine-tuned models, and the combined
ALL model retains 0.13 while keeping the lowest overall WER. Zero-shot Whisper
large-v3 preserves English words best of all (0.65) but at a far higher overall
WER (0.91), since it captures neither dialect phonology nor a coherent
transcript. This
confirms that seq2seq models with a shared multilingual vocabulary, fine-tuned
on code-switched data, handle CS words far more faithfully than monolingual
CTC models.

\paragraph{Substitution patterns.}
The most frequent substitution errors involve:
(1)~dialect-specific vowels and consonants being substituted with their
standard-Bengali equivalents;
(2)~English technical terms being transcribed in Bengali script
(transliteration rather than transcription); and
(3)~short English function words (``is'', ``the'', ``in'') being deleted.

\paragraph{Per-dialect difficulty.}
The most phonologically distinct dialect, Sylhet (0.93 WER even after
fine-tuning), is consistently the hardest across all models, followed by the
coastal/island varieties Sandwip and Old~Dhaka. Central dialects (Khulna,
Mymensingh, Narail, Noakhali) are the easiest, reaching 0.66--0.70 WER. The
proposed model is best or tied on 14 of 15 dialects; only on Barishal does the
larger zero-shot Whisper large-v3 win (0.82 vs.\ 0.87), indicating that model
scale still matters for some dialects and motivating larger fine-tuned
backbones as future work.

\subsection{CS vs.\ BN\_ONLY Difficulty Gap}

Table~\ref{tab:cs_gap} reports WER on CS vs.\ BN\_ONLY clips. The whole-clip gap
is small (within $\pm0.12$): because code-switched clips are predominantly
Bengali, code-switching does not substantially inflate overall clip WER.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\label{tab:cs_gap}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule

\midrule
Whisper large-v3 (0-shot)   & 0.80 & 0.92 & $-$0.12 \\
Whisper-small (0-shot)      & 1.02 & 1.07 & $-$0.05 \\
MMS-1B                      & 0.94 & 0.99 & $-$0.05 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Rather, the difficulty of code-switching is concentrated in the \emph{English
tokens} themselves. As Table~\ref{tab:error_types} shows, English-word survival
is near zero for monolingual and BN\_ONLY models and English-word WER approaches
1.0, even though overall clip WER barely changes. Code-switching difficulty is
therefore real but \emph{localised}---it manifests as the systematic loss of
English words, not as a higher overall clip WER. Fine-tuning on code-switched
data is what recovers these English tokens (CS-F1 and EN-survival in
Tables~\ref{tab:main_results} and~\ref{tab:error_types}).

Of the 8{,}712 borderline clips, the Qwen2.5 judge accepted 1{,}727 (19.8\%)

passed to dataset construction.

\subsection{LLM-Ensemble Evaluation}

Beyond string-level metrics, we evaluate ASR hypotheses with a three-model
LLM-as-a-judge ensemble (Mistral-7B~\citep{jiang2023mistral},
LLaMA-3.1-8B~\citep{dubey2024llama}, and Gemma~\citep{team2024gemma}), each run
locally via Ollama. Every model scores the hypothesis against the reference on
three 0--5 dimensions---semantic correctness, code-switch preservation, and
dialectal naturalness---and the ensemble reports the mean score per dimension
together with an inter-model agreement rate. This metric captures meaning-
preserving variation (e.g.\ \textit{miting}/\textit{meeting}) and silent
dropping of English code-switch words, which WER penalises or misses.

\subsection{Statistical Significance}

All WER improvements of the proposed system (Whisper FT ALL) over the baselines
are statistically significant at $p < 0.001$ (paired bootstrap, $N=10{,}000$).
The 95\% confidence interval for the proposed system's overall WER is
[0.79, 0.81], which does not overlap the interval of any baseline (e.g.\
zero-shot Whisper large-v3, [0.90, 0.92]). The effect sizes are statistically
robust but numerically small (Cohen's~$d = 0.09$ vs.\ large-v3), as expected
when comparing models that all operate in a high-error regime on this
challenging benchmark.
